# DECISIONS

Format: `D-NNN (date) — decision. Rationale. Impact on claims.`

- **D-001 (2026-10-24) — Fallback rungs: sim-only robot, Apple MPS/CPU compute, synthetic phone videos.** No arm, no NVIDIA GPU, and no phone videos exist yet. Rationale: the research question can still be posed and answered at the `sim` stage. Impact: every headline number is labelled `sim`. The video-vs-teleop and active-request answers are *simulation evidence only* until the sessions in SESSION_QUEUE.md are run.
- **D-002 (2026-10-24) — Own SO-100-class MJCF instead of a downloaded model.** Link lengths approximate the SO-100 (0.113 m / 0.135 m / 0.10 m TCP), with a 5-DoF chain + parallel jaw and a closed-form IK (`common/kinematics.py`). Rationale: self-contained, testable, and no licence/network dependency. Impact: sim kinematics ≠ a calibrated real unit. Real link lengths come from calibration (M1).
- **D-003 (2026-10-24) — Grasp = physical finger contact + kinematic attachment.** Fingers collide with objects, so imprecise approaches push objects. A grasp only *holds* when the object passes a geometric jaw-containment test at closing time (`TidyEnv.grasp_check`). Rationale: reliable grasp physics in MuJoCo needs heavy tuning, and this standard shortcut keeps grasp *precision* (position, yaw, width) as the thing being tested. Impact: sim success overestimates real success for slippery/heavy objects. Grasp-force failures are not modelled.
- **D-004 (2026-10-24) — Gravity compensation on arm links in sim.** Real STS3215-class servos hold pose under gravity with an internal PID. Without gravcomp our position servos sagged ~1.7 cm at full reach. Impact: none on claims. Real sag is measured in the repeatability test (M1).
- **D-005 (2026-10-24) — Policies use low-dimensional, object-centric observations** (proprio + perceived target-object pose + place-target pose, all relative to the TCP), not raw images. Rationale: CPU/MPS budget, 10-demo data regime, and the same observation is available on the real robot from the overhead camera perception. Impact: results do not test end-to-end visuomotor learning. Perception errors enter through measured noise (M3) and are swept.
- **D-006 (2026-10-24) — Action space: TCP position delta + jaw yaw (π-symmetric) + tool pitch + gripper, 10 Hz, chunked.** IK and the safety filter run below the policy, so a learned policy can never command a joint outside the safety limits.
- **D-007 (2026-10-24) — The scripted controller (`sim/expert.py`) is used only as a *simulated teleoperator* and for env tests.** It is never reported as a learned policy.
- **D-008 (2026-10-24) — Grasp hold switched from kinematic teleport to a soft weld (activated at the jaw-containment test). Free-object horizontal speed is clamped to 0.35 m/s and angular speed to 10 rad/s. Rolling friction is on (condim 6).** Rationale: the kinematic teleport, and squeezing light objects between a stiff servo and the table, ejected objects at unphysical speeds (traced: a cup slid 1.6 m). Impact: sim cannot reproduce fast tumbling. It slightly under-states "knocked off the table" failures.
- **D-009 (2026-10-24) — Continuous gripper opening command.** Demonstrators and the teleoperator open the jaw only ~2–3.5 cm wider than the object, not fully. Rationale: a fully opened jaw (8 cm) knocked neighbouring items off the shelf/bin, and careful humans and operators don't do this. Video retargeting gets the width directly from the thumb–index distance.
- **D-010 (2026-10-24) — Scripted/teleop motion rises vertically to a 12 cm transit height before horizontal moves, and moves in (radius, pan, z).** Rationale: traced failures where the fingers swept through just-placed items, and straight lines crossed the base no-go column (correctly held by the safety filter → timeouts).
- **D-011 (2026-10-24) — Workspace fixtures resized before any result was produced:** bins 15×12 cm (the 11.4 cm pen did not fit the old bin), shelf lowered to 2.5 cm with shorter items, tape radius 3.0 cm, and per-item drop spots in bins. The evaluation protocol was re-frozen after these changes. **No result uses an earlier protocol** (checked: results/ contains only the safety-smoke log).
- **D-012 (2026-10-24) — Simulated-teleop feasibility check (not a headline result):** the simulated operator completes most chores in this twin. The per-chore operator success during data collection is recorded in every M4/M7 result file (`meta.operator_success`).
- **D-013 (2026-10-24) — Synthetic human demonstrator and simulated hand estimator (B-003 fallback).** Human motion = minimum-jerk segments (peak 0.4–0.9 m/s), oblique approach from the human side (25–60° from vertical), pinch across the narrow dimension, 1–2 mm tremor, handheld-phone shake. The 3D hand keypoints used for learning come from a *simulated* HaMeR/WiLoR-class estimator: GT keypoints + AR(1) camera-frame noise (σ_xy 6 mm, σ_z 12 mm), emitted only on frames where skin pixels are visible. The noise level is an assumption, so M3/M4 report a sweep. Fingertip-cap colour detection is the alternative `caps` hand source. Impact: M3 numbers are `sim`. Real hand-pose error is unmeasured until filming.
- **D-014 (2026-10-26) — Camera pose by tracked PnP.** Full 4-marker solves where possible, then forward/backward tracking with ≥3 markers matched to the previous pose's projection (jump-gated), then hold-last. Rationale: one mat corner is often occluded by an object or the hand, which left 12–86 % of frames solved; tracking raises it to 80–100 % (development diagnostic, not a reported result). The real system uses ArUco IDs, where correspondences come for free.
- **D-015 (2026-10-26) — Release time = first frame at the bottom of the hand's place motion (smoothed grasp-centre height, near the settled object), with aperture opening as the fallback.** Rationale: with σ_z/σ_xy-level keypoint noise the aperture-threshold cue was 0.5 s early to 3 s late. Height-minimum cut the release-time error spread substantially on 24 sim clips (development diagnostic, tuned on `film` clips only and never on eval; the reported event metrics are in `results/sim/m3_perception.json`).
- **D-016 (2026-10-26) — Program inference: landmark positions at place time come from where the other objects came to rest (place position if carried earlier, else rest position), not from occluded frames. Pick order is enforced only by landmark dependencies (A placed on B ⇒ B first) or ≥90 % pairwise precedence agreement over ≥5 demos.** Identical instances (block_red / block_red2) are interchangeable for scoring.
- **D-017 (2026-10-26) — Bug fix in the synthetic demonstrator:** the hand path is planned before rendering, so object-relative goals (stacking) used the landmark object's *start* pose. The demonstrator now tracks where it has put each object. No result had been produced with the bug (results/ held only the safety smoke).
- **D-018 (2026-10-26) — Session-start colour calibration for the overhead perceiver.** The hue of each colour class is measured once per session from a reference image (real robot: the operator clicks each object once; sim: segmentation render). Rationale: lighting variants shift rendered hues enough to break fixed thresholds. Impact: the lighting generalization test measures the calibrated system, not a fixed-threshold one.
- **D-019 (2026-10-26) — Sim pre-training data = task-agnostic random pick-and-place in the twin** (random objects from the library, random goals on the mat/bins/shelf/tray, seeds disjoint from eval), generated by the scripted planner. It is never chore-specific, so it does not leak the task program.
- **D-020 (2026-10-26) — Object-centric snapping in retargeting:** grasp xy = tracked object rest position when the hand grasp centre is within 3 cm; jaw axis snaps to the nearer object axis; place xy = tracked rest position when within 5 cm. The `hand` variant (ablation "−object-centric") keeps hand-only targets.
- **D-021 (2026-10-27, revised) — Keyframe corrective augmentation (`policies/augment.py`) implemented, OFF by default; it is applied identically to a data source when enabled.** Re-measured with the final policy configuration (`results/sim/dev_policy_design_desk_clearing.json`, validation cards): it **hurts** video pseudo-demo policies but **helps** teleop policies. An earlier development note ("hurts both") came from the old delta-chunk configuration and was wrong for the final one. Because helping the baseline is the fair choice, M4 adds `teleop_*_aug` conditions (the teleop baseline with its best validated configuration), reported next to the shared-configuration teleop rows (`results/sim/m4_teleop_augmented_desk_clearing.json`). Likely reason for the asymmetry: teleop demos have human-like wobble and hesitation, so straight-line corrections add useful coverage. Video pseudo-demos are already clean robot-speed paths, so the synthetic corrections conflict with their approach profile.
- **D-022 (2026-10-27) — Pseudo-demo goal features are relabelled with the inferred task program** (`bench.common.segments_from_records`). Before the fix, a bin goal in the video pseudo-demos was the bin *floor* (where the object came to rest), but at runtime it is the bin *rim* (release height): a 4 cm systematic train/test mismatch that only affected video data. No headline result used the old labels.
- **D-023 (2026-10-27) — Executor "ready pose" primitive before every object step** (open the jaw part-way if closed, rise vertically to transit height). It is identical for all agents and data sources, and recorded teleop demos pass through it too. Rationale: traced cascades where one failed step left the arm low with a closed jaw next to other objects, a state no demo contains. Its ticks count toward episode time.
- **D-024 (2026-10-27) — Default observation = `minimal`:** the object-relative and goal-relative position, object yaw, class and size features, and gripper opening/command. The absolute TCP position, jaw yaw and pitch are dropped, and pitch is set by the executor's kinematics (`preferred_pitch`). Rationale: with 10–40 demos the absolute features let the network memorise trajectories. Evidence: `results/sim/dev_policy_design_desk_clearing.json` (rows cumulative_rel vs cumulative_minimal). Upright cylinders have no observed yaw (the perceived yaw is noise).
- **D-025 (2026-10-27) — Cumulative action chunks (the default):** chunk entry k holds the TCP target of action t+k relative to the TCP at time t, as in ACT's absolute-position chunks. Temporal ensembling therefore averages *spatial targets*. With per-tick deltas, the chunk encoded the demonstrator's *timing*: whenever the arm lagged (under the servo model it moves only part of the commanded step per tick), the policy decelerated and descended 3–5 cm short of the object (traced). Evidence: `results/sim/dev_policy_design_desk_clearing.json` (rows delta_rel, delta_rel_newest, cumulative_rel).
- **D-026 (2026-10-27) — Hyper-parameters chosen on validation cards only** (`pool` split, seeds disjoint from eval), averaged over the video and teleop data sources so neither method is favoured. ACT K = 10, 3000 steps, batch 128, lr 1e-3, perception-noise augmentation σ = 0 (vs 4 mm). DP did not train to a usable policy at the same 3000-step budget. Evidence for all of these is re-measured in `results/sim/dev_policy_design_desk_clearing.json`; the development-time scratch numbers are not reported anywhere. It is reported as such, and ACT is the primary policy class (Rule 6: equal compute per method).
- **D-027 (2026-10-27) — Two bugs found in the first M7 run (results discarded, never reported).** (1) *Condition-key collision:* trained policies were keyed `name@seed` without the chore, so in the multi-chore run each chore was evaluated with an "ensemble" of policies trained on four different chores (every trial scored 0, including teleop). Fixed: the key is now `chore/name@seed`. Single-chore experiments (M4, M5, M6, design ablation) were unaffected because each runs one chore. (2) *Same-colour fixtures:* the sorting bins are tinted red and blue, so the overhead perceiver segmented the red bin as the red block. Fixed with a size-plausibility gate (a component must not be much larger than its object class, whose size comes from the object library; `perception.pipeline.size_plausible`). The gate is applied in the runtime overhead perceiver. Verified behaviour-neutral for desk clearing: identical outcomes on all 40 `eval_seen` trials of the M4 video_10 seed-0 checkpoint. The video pipeline already uses a stricter saturation threshold that excludes the tinted bins, so it is left unchanged, and the desk video dataset hash is unchanged.
